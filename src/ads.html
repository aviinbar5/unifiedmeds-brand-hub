Paid rules.

The rules on Voice and compliance, applied to paid media.

Headline of nine words or fewer.

It must work without the image and with the sound off.

Everything critical inside the safe area.

Platforms crop and lay their own buttons over the creative. The dashed areas below mark what stays clear, per channel.

The legal line lives in the creative.

"Assessment only. Not a diagnosis." inside the ad or the video's end card, never only in the caption (R-63).

One CTA: "Get started".

Yellow, the only Yellow in the unit, a rectangle with 5px corners (R-11, R-55).

Two shapes per surface, at most.

The capsule (true shape, both halves and the divider, like the seal) or Quadrant pieces; on a photo the logo icon counts as one of the two (R-106, R-110).

The capsule on a photo.

Paper line on a dark or busy photo, Deep Teal on a light one, the long half filled at the soft opacity. It enters from an edge, toward the subject, never over a face or under text (R-110).

No drug names, no claims.

No prescription name, benefit, price, statistic, review, result or before/after in any ad (R-58, R-61, R-65, R-67).

No personal attributes.

Never "Do you have…", "your condition" or anything that says what the viewer has. Meta and Google reject it, and it breaks discretion.

Health ad policy.

Prescription treatment and telehealth are restricted categories on every paid channel. Nothing runs until the account is certified and legal has signed the creative.

Blocker · legal

Meta and Google both require LegitScript certification (and their own approval) before ads for prescription treatment or telehealth can run. This is the first item before any campaign. Policies differ by country and change; every line below is [check] with legal.

The same five beats everywhere.

Hook, the assessment, the provider, the button with the legal line, the end card. Each channel changes the frame, the safe zone and the length, never the message.

Captions on, always.

Burned-in Paper caption boxes (5px corners, Deep Teal type) inside the channel's safe zone, on every spoken line, even where sound is usually on.

Colour in ads.

Six sanctioned pairings, each with its contrast. One ground, one type colour, at most one accent (Green or Blue) and the Yellow button. Rotate pairings across a campaign so a series feels varied without leaving the brand.

Don't

Static.

Type-led units for feed and link placements on Meta, LinkedIn and X. True proportions, built from the tokens. Headlines are candidates from the line bank.

Feed versions: 4:5 and 1:1.

The same five beats, recomposed: captions inside the centre, the seal in the top left corner, the end card centred. Cut to 6 seconds (beats 01, 04, 05) for a short version.

Sound.

Music bed and the sting sound from the Videos page. A voice-over repeats the captions word for word; it never adds a claim the captions do not show.

Creator-style (UGC).

Phone-native ads that look like a person's own post, still on brand: Paper caption boxes with 5px corners, the seal small, one Yellow button at the end.

Legal review

Only with a real creator, written consent and a disclosed paid partnership (Meta adds the "Paid partnership" label). Creators talk about the process, never about results, symptoms or a product name. No testimonial runs until legal approves it (R-67).

Reels and Stories · 9:16

Carousel.

Four 1:1 cards, one step each: the four steps of How it works, in the same words as the website and the landing page. One capsule outline runs across the cards so the set reads as one piece. The last card carries the button and the legal line.

Story carousel.

Up to three story frames in one ad on Facebook and Instagram [check]. Each frame stands alone (people tap away), but the set tells the steps in order. The button and the legal line are on every frame; the shapes stay the same across the set.

16:9 video: YouTube in-stream and bumper.

Skippable in-stream ads must land the brand and the message before the skip button at 5 seconds. A 6 second bumper is one beat. The bottom corners belong to YouTube (ad info and skip). The same frames serve X, LinkedIn and desktop feeds.

In-stream · 16:9 · 15 seconds

Bumper · 6 seconds.

One frame of copy over a photo or Deep Teal, the logo icon in the corner, ending on the seal. No button inside the creative: YouTube's overlay carries it.

Mobile.

Most YouTube views are on a phone: type at least 50px on the 1080 high master, lines aligned left, nothing in the bottom 18%.

Display banners.

Google Display Network. Prefer responsive display (Google assembles our image, logo and lines); keep these fixed sizes for direct buys. Logo, one line, the button and the legal line; no photo below 300 wide. Shown scaled to the column; exact sizes in the captions.

Weight.

Each file under 150 KB [check]. Animated HTML5 runs once, 15 s at most, and ends on the button frame; never a loop.

No shapes under 300 wide.

At banner size the capsule's step cannot read (R-109); the logo is the only capsule.

LinkedIn: single image and document.

A professional feed: calm, type-led, no faces needed. Use LinkedIn for brand, provider recruiting and partnerships until legal confirms health advertising there [check].

In context.

One representative ad per category, in a desktop browser and on a phone. The surrounding interface is generic and grey on purpose: it is not any platform's real screen. Check the real placement preview in each ads manager before launch.

Reduced motion.

Where a platform or the viewer asks for less motion (banners, our own site), every frame cuts and every element is already in place. Video ads keep their cuts and drop the moves.

Never.

Spinning or bouncing logos, a pulsing button, a shimmer, flashing more than 3 times a second, or text that moves while it is read.
