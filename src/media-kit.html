Who we are.

The boilerplate in three lengths, in US English for the press. Built from the positioning on the Who we are page and the approved landing page; the wording follows the glossary (provider, assessment, member, R-57). Use the text unchanged.

25 words

unifiedmeds is online men's health care. A man completes an assessment, a licensed U.S. provider reviews it, and if treatment is appropriate, it ships discreetly.

50 words

unifiedmeds is a men's health telehealth brand launching with Sexual health. A man completes a short online assessment. A licensed U.S. provider reviews it and decides whether treatment is right for him. If it is, a licensed U.S. pharmacy prepares and ships it in plain packaging. Care continues after delivery.

100 words · the "About unifiedmeds" paragraph

unifiedmeds is a men's health telehealth brand. It launches with Sexual health, and Longevity and Hair loss are planned to follow. A man starts with a short online assessment, from home, on his own schedule. A licensed U.S. provider reviews his answers, may ask follow-up questions, and decides whether treatment is right for him. If it is, a licensed U.S. pharmacy prepares the prescription and ships it in plain packaging, with nothing on the outside about what is inside. Care continues after delivery: the member can message his care team, and a provider reviews every refill. The brand speaks plainly.

Logo pack.

The capsule seal in three lockups, each for light grounds (Navy) and for dark grounds and photos (reversed, R-91). SVG wherever possible; PNG with a transparent background at 800 px wide for screens and 2400 px for print and large screens.

The capsule is not symmetric: the left half is longer (55% of the length) and the right half is taller. That is the logo, not an error. Never redraw it, never rebuild it as an even pill, never retype the wordmark. Use the files.

Clear space

The height of the "meds" half on every side (R-92). Nothing enters it: no text, no other logo, no edge of the frame or the photo.

Three things never to do

Never stretch, squash or rotate it.

Scale it only by its corners, with the proportions locked. No shadows, outlines or effects.

Never recolour it.

Navy on light grounds, reversed on dark grounds and photos. Black or white only for technical uses (fax, emboss, stamp), with the one-colour files. No other colours, no single parts recoloured (R-91, R-92, R-94).

The only seal: the file.

Photos that carry the brand

Three ways a photo carries unifiedmeds, each in the four screen formats it runs in, side by side at one height. The capsule is the true logo shape: two halves with the divider between them and the step at the joint, its stroke 0.85% of its length, the long half filled at 50% (R-109, R-110). It enters from a frame edge and stays off faces; at most two shapes per photo, the stamp included. The stamp is the logo icon, the u|m mark without its Navy tile: its long side is 12% of the image's short side (130 px on a 1080 image), 1.5% of the short side from the edges (16 px on a 1080 image), Paper on dark or busy photos and Deep Teal on light ones. When a phone crop would put a shape on the person, the shape moves (see the 9:16 of the first set). Files: 05-images-PLACEHOLDER/branded/ in the ZIP.

Writing about unifiedmeds.

For journalists, editors and partners. It follows the wording rules in brand-rules.md (R-07, R-57, R-65, R-67) and the Voice, content and compliance page.

The name: unifiedmeds.

All lowercase, one word, also at the start of a sentence and in a headline. No trademark sign in running text.

unifiedmeds
Never another spelling.

Not with a capital, not split, not in capitals.

Press release template.

The structure every release follows. Square brackets are the parts to write; legal reads the release before it goes out. On a phone the template wraps as one column; nothing scrolls sideways.

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE   or   EMBARGOED UNTIL [Month Day, Year, time, time zone]

[Headline: one plain sentence, sentence case, ending with a full stop. No medicine name, no claim, no number.]

[Subhead: one sentence that says what is new and for whom.]

[CITY, State], [Month Day, Year]. unifiedmeds, [one-line description from the 25-word boilerplate], today [announced / launched / opened] [what, in plain words].

[Paragraph 2: what changes for men, and how care works: online assessment, review by a licensed U.S. provider, a licensed U.S. pharmacy, plain packaging. Say "if treatment is appropriate".]

[Paragraph 3: availability. Where: [States served · operations]. When: [date]. Price only if legal and operations approve it.]

"[Quote, two sentences at most, approved by legal.]" said [Name, title · only if approved].
