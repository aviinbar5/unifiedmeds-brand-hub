How a new product gets its name.

A single medicine is always called by the medicine. A combination gets a short descriptive name, like Combo.

  1. Brief.What is in it, the form, the category, who it is for. The clinical team confirms the facts.
  2. Long list.Plain descriptive candidates. One English word, one or two syllables, easy to say on the phone, one line on a 390 wide screen.
  3. Screen.Remove any word on the never list, any word that names the body, a symptom or a result, and any word that reveals the category on a mailer.
  4. Check.Trademark search (USPTO, class 5 and 44), existing drug names (FDA), domain and social handles, and no clash with a sound-alike medicine.
  5. Legal.Legal and compliance approve the short list in writing, including the line under the name.
  6. Avi decides.One name. It goes into brand-rules, the label family (a new capsule colour, R-97) and the product tokens.

Words that never go in a name.

Results and speed.

Max, Power, Ultra, Instant, Rock, Last, Boost, Strong, Pro. A name is not a promise (R-108).

The body and the condition.

Anything anatomical, ED, Erect, Stiff, any pun on the condition. Discretion first (R-108).

Other brands.

Cialis, Viagra or any reference brand, any “-like” or “generic of” naming, any sound-alike of a medicine (R-108).
