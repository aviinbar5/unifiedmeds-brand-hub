Photography shows ordinary life.

Real people in real places, in natural light. Nothing clinical, nothing staged for effect. A replacement photo passes every rule on this page.

Casting

  • Men aged 35 to 65 lead hero and product imagery (R-37).
  • Partners appear in Sexual health stories: relaxed and natural, never suggestive.
  • Service and support sections (care team, delivery, member support) use a balanced, modular mix of people of all genders (R-37, U-27).
  • Women lead only in future women's products Planned.
  • Real ages and real bodies. No gym physiques.
  • Unposed, doing something. Eye contact optional, never intense.

Light and setting

  • Natural, available light. True colour; no teal-and-orange grade, no duotone.
  • Home, bathroom and outdoors, as in Figma 2708-1034.
  • Shoot wide with a clean corner, so a kit piece or copy has somewhere to land.
  • Closed, branded bottles are allowed (R-38; Figma Packaging · Labels, 03 · Bottle mockups).

Faces

  • A face is never covered by a shape, by text or by the crop (R-110).
  • One exception: at most one faceless detail crop per page, such as the neck-and-hands crop in the Figma category hero (R-38).

Never in a photo.

Rules R-38 and R-39.

  • Loose pills or open packs
  • Syringes
  • Lab coats, scrubs, stethoscopes, clinics
  • Before and after pairs
  • Covered faces, beyond the one detail crop per page
  • Watermarked stock on the website, in ads or in press. Inside the hubs only, a watermarked paid preview may stand in, with a small “Buy this image” link and the tag “Not approved · to replace” (R-39, U-27)
  • A model captioned as a member, for example “Member · 47”
  • A photo of unknown source or licence. The earlier photos of unknown source have left the site (U-31)

Copy and photos: three ways.

Copy may sit beside the photo, on a solid colour panel, or directly on the photo. All three are allowed (R-120); the third only where the photo leaves a calm area and the copy passes contrast there.

Current · R-120

Legibility rules for copy directly on a photo. The copy sits on a calm area of the photo (a wall, a window, the sky, the floor), never across a face or skin, never on a shape (R-33, R-110). Contrast is measured on the area under the letters, at every crop: 4.5:1 for text under 24px, 3:1 for DM Serif at 24px and up (R-72). Teal Deep or Deep Teal on light areas, Paper on dark ones. No scrim, gradient or blur: there is no token for one; if the area is not calm enough, use the panel or set the copy beside the photo. On a photo, a headline and one short line at most; buttons use Outline Light, which hovers to Blue-dark on photos (R-41). On a phone the crop must keep the calm area; if it does not, the copy moves below the photo.

Shapes on photos.

A photo may carry the logo seal as a small stamp, or one or two brand shapes: the true capsule (outline, long half translucent) or Quadrant Kit pieces. They enter from an edge, stay light, and never touch a face or sit under text.

Current · R-109, R-110

Outline stroke 0.85% of the capsule's length, minimum 1.5px, and at every size the two halves keep the divider, like the seal (R-109): the long half open toward the joint, the short, taller half closed by the divider, the step where the heights meet. The translucent long half sits inside the line with a small gap. Paper on dark or busy photos, Deep Teal on light ones; the long half filled in Paper or Wash Blue at half opacity (--opacity-capsule-soft). Enter from an edge or corner, cropped by it, toward the subject. Two shapes per photo at most, the stamp included (R-106). The stamp is the logo icon: the u|m mark from the app icon without its Navy tile (files assets/shapes/logo-stamp-paper.svg and logo-stamp-teal.svg). Paper on dark or busy photos, Deep Teal on light ones; 48px on its long side on desktop, 40px on a phone, never smaller, 16px from the edges. Shapes are drawn as inline SVG from tools/capsule_svg.py geometry, sized in % of the photo so they scale with it.

Alt text.

Every photo carries alt text that describes it. The same rules on desktop and on a phone.

  • Describe what the photo shows in one plain sentence. Do not start with “Image of”.
  • Describe this photo, not the one it replaced. Update the alt text whenever the image changes.
  • No claims, and no feelings the photo does not show.
  • Product images are content: name the product, for example “unifiedmeds Tadalafil bottle, closed”.
  • Use an empty alt="" only when the photo is purely decorative.
