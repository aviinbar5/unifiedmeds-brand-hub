Voice, content and compliance.

How unifiedmeds sounds, the words we use, the lines every page must carry, and what we do not publish until legal approves it. brand-rules.md wins on compliance and wording; this page explains it and gives the examples.

Calm, plain, private and respectful.

unifiedmeds is a men’s health telehealth brand that launches with Sexual health. The voice is masculine, on the gentle side: direct and brief, never loud, never clinical for its own sake.

Masculine, on the gentle side.

Write like a calm, competent man who respects the reader: second person, present tense, short sentences with full stops. Say the awkward thing plainly, once, and move on. No bravado, no locker-room words, no hype.

Two registers, always in this order.

The human sentence first, then the precise one. Never one without the other. This carries over from Edition 01 unchanged.

Human · DM Serif Display

You don’t have to figure it out alone.

Names a feeling. One line. No number, no promise.

Precise · Inter

A licensed provider will review your health information and determine which option, if any, is appropriate for you.

Names who does what. “May” and “can” only where clinically true.

Both lines are current Sexual health page copy (Figma 2708-1034).

Private.

Discretion is part of the care. We say what stays private, and we never promise more privacy than the service delivers.

A question headline, a clinical acronym up front and a privacy promise nobody can verify.

Respectful.

The reader is an adult who decides. We never frame him by what is wrong with him, and we never decide for him.

Sentence case everywhere.

Buttons, nav items, headings, eyebrows, labels and table headers are all in sentence case. Labels use the label style (--type-desktop-label-*, 12/16 SemiBold, +2%) with no uppercase transform. Proper names keep their capitals, except the brand name, which is always lowercase (unifiedmeds): Sexual health (the category), Better Intimacy (the editorial title), Combo.

One exception: the compliance marker “DEMO ONLY” stays in capitals, because it must be impossible to miss.

Headlines end with a full stop and are never questions.

The rule covers heroes and section headlines. It does not cover button labels, nav labels, legal document titles, FAQ questions, or the label-style module title “Frequently asked questions”.

Assessment and picker prompts may be questions, because the question is the headline of that step. They are set in DM Serif Display 26/1.3 (--type-desktop-h3-*).

Heroes: no prices, no symptoms, no benefit adjectives.

A hero carries a human line, a short subhead and the call to action. Experiences are described in plain words below the hero, not in it. Prices live in the product buy box. Claims such as durations or comparisons live in the product body, next to the safety information.

This

“Feel more like yourself.”

Homepage hero, Figma homepage 6/10

Not this

“Difficulty getting or keeping an erection? … As low as $6 a pill.”

A symptom question and a price in a hero. Both are in current site copy, to fix.

Numbers carry a unit, and a range where one exists.

  • Never a bare number. Write “about 1 hour”, “$36 a month”, “20 mg” (a space before the unit).
  • A result value always shows its reference range next to it. Never summarize a result without showing it.
  • Durations and frequencies for a medication (“up to 36 hours”) appear only on its product page, in the body, with the safety information on the same page.
  • Every statistic needs a source and legal approval first. See Compliance.

Microcopy library.

The short words of the site and the product: buttons, labels, errors, empty states, confirmations. One voice on every screen. Square brackets are values the product fills in. The developer can import every line from microcopy.json, keyed as in the last column.

Sentence case.

Buttons, labels, menus and headings: “Get started”, never “Get Started” (R-24).

Verb first, short.

A button says what happens, in one to three words.

No “please”, no “!”.

Plain and calm. The interface is not asking a favour.

Say what to do next.

Every error says how to fix it, in one sentence, without blame.

Member, not patient.

In the product the person is a member (R-57).

No health detail in passing.

Toasts, emails and notifications never name a condition, a medicine or a result.

Words we don’t use.

Claims and hype

cure · fix · guaranteed · clinically proven · miracle · best · number one · instant · real results · doctor-approved · FDA-approved (for a compounded medication)

Bravado

alpha · beast · prime · peak performance · unlock your potential · optimize your… · biohack · reverse aging

Clinical shorthand up front

ED, PE or “premature ejaculation” in heroes, ads, nav or email subjects. On the category and product pages, below the hero, write “erectile dysfunction (ED)” on first use. Never “Low T” as a diagnosis.

Retired lines

Tired isn’t a personality · “Fine” isn’t a baseline · Off isn’t a diagnosis · Something changed. Start with answers. · Men’s longevity, made simple.

Compliance.

What stays off every surface until legal approves real data, where prescription names may appear, and how prices are shown. This applies in demos as in production.

Not allowed until legal approves real data.

Press strip

“As featured in” logo walls, trust badges, seals and certifications.

Clinical statistics and success rates

Count-up stats, percentages, “works for most men”.

Reviews and star ratings

Rating summaries, star rows, review counts, review carousels.

Testimonials presented as real

Invented quotes, and any “Verified patient” label. No verification process exists.

Before and after

In words or in images.

Invented clinicians

Named doctors, credentials or headshots that are not real, approved people.

Unsourced statistics

“1 in 4 men over 45…”, “Most men wait four years…”.

Stated turnaround

“Within one working day”. Write “reviewed promptly” until operations confirm a service level.

Before any of these ships.

Real, sourced data on file; legal approval with a date and an owner; written consent from any person quoted; disclosure if they were compensated; no outcome claims. Illustrative quotes in prototypes carry the DEMO ONLY marker and the attribution “Member · age”, never “Verified”.

Privacy.

Health information is handled on the clinical side only. The marketing site informs and routes; it does not collect.

  • The marketing site never collects health data. No health questions in its forms. The newsletter asks for an email address only. The contact form asks people not to include medical details.
  • Assessment answers live only on the clinical intake domain. A picker on the marketing site only links to the intake. It never stores an answer or puts it in a URL, a cookie or an analytics event.
  • No tracking pixels on condition or product pages. No third-party ad tags, pixels or session replay on the Sexual health category, product pages, the assessment or the member portal.
  • Consent before any analytics. A consent banner appears before any analytics runs. Banner wording, tools and scope: To confirm
